abstract: "Fiji aims to achieve 100% renewable electricity by 2030, a target that is rapidly approaching and demands immediate action. To explore realistic pathways toward this goal, we modelled Fiji’s future electricity system using two tools: HOMER and PyPSA-Earth. We compared the results to understand how each model represents the energy transition, highlighting differences in their approaches to system design. PyPSA-Earth includes hydro power dispatchability and supports multiple grid nodes, capturing spatial variation across the electricity network, whereas HOMER uses a simpler, single-node approach with high technical resolution on limited technologies. These differences explain the variations in technology mix and investment needs across the two models. Our findings from both frameworks show that a significant increase in renewable capacity is required. Specifically, HOMER expects almost 0.2 GW of installed solar photovoltaic (PV) power and 0.13 GW of wind capacity. Additionally, HOMER almost doubled the biomass generation capacity to 0.1 GW to meet Fiji’s 2030 renewable electricity target. PyPSA-Earth also has similar expectations, with a higher inclination toward cheaper solar PV power, increasing the installed capacity to 0.7 GW, and projecting 0.06 GW installations for both onshore wind and biomass. Moreover, the economic analysis indicates that these transition pathways not only meet the 2030 target but also lead to significant reductions in the levelised cost of electricity."
